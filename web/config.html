Flight Tracker Setup

Set your WiFi network, home location, and which flights to show on your flight tracker over a USB cable.

This browser can't talk to USB devices

Setup uses the Web Serial feature, which is available in Chrome and Edge on a computer (not Safari, Firefox, or phones). Open this page in one of those browsers.

1. Connect your flight tracker

Plug the flight tracker into this computer with a USB cable, then press Connect. In the list that opens, choose the entry named USB Serial (some boards show CP210x or CH340 instead). It only appears while the board is plugged in.

Don't see it in the list? Show all serial ports.

2. WiFi network

Type your network name, or press Scan to pick from nearby networks (takes about 10 seconds). 2.4 GHz networks only.

Home location

Where the flight tracker itself is - distances and directions on screen are measured from here.

"Use my current location" asks your browser, which on a laptop often guesses from your internet connection - check the result, or use an address below.

Sent to OpenStreetMap's Nominatim service, only when you press the button. Include the city and state or country.

What to show

Origin/destination are optional - set them to lock in a flight's route if the tracker ever shows a stale or wrong one for it.

Display

On the tracker itself: tap the left/right edge of the screen to cycle these; tap the top/bottom edge to page through flights that don't fit.

Radar screen

How far out the radar screen's rings reach - independent of the search radius above, which still controls what's fetched at all. A smaller range zooms in for a less crowded view.

Each shows as a "+" and its code on the radar screen, at its real location.

Firmware

Installed: connect the flight tracker above to read its version. You can also install without connecting.

No firmware comes with this page yet. Build it with pio run -e esp32dev, then reload.

Your WiFi, location, flights and display settings are kept when you update.
